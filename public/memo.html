Section 1

01Executive Summary & the $140M Agency Benchmark

Signet verifies client-assistance payments made from restricted public grants so that two named people can certify and release them in minutes, with an audit record an auditor can read without the vendor present. One language-model role — document parsing — and no model authority. That boundary is the product.

Public human-services agencies pay third parties — landlords, utilities, phone carriers, the DMV — on behalf of individuals out of dozens of restricted grants simultaneously. Each grant carries its own eligibility, per-instance and per-client caps, grant period, prohibited line items, and reporting format. The dollars per payment are small; the transaction count and the audit exposure are large. The work of deciding whether a given payment is allowable, allocable, and documented lives today in people reading documents and in email — not in any system of record. Grants-management software records the award; accounting software records the payment; the compliance judgment between them is manual.

Signet occupies that gap. It is not a grants-management suite, a case-management system, or a bank. It is one function, executed end to end: request → document parsing → policy verification → bank match → human certification → human release → compliance audit log → funder report. The system's only machine-produced outcomes are READY and ESCALATED. It never approves, never denies, and never moves money.

The $140M agency benchmark

The reference operating environment is a county youth & family services department with an annual budget on the order of $140M, administering 30–50 restricted grant streams (assumption; the synthetic workload models eight representative streams in depth). Audit mechanics: 2 CFR 200 and the cost of a disallowed cost

Every federal dollar an agency spends is governed by the Uniform Guidance (2 CFR Part 200). Costs must be allowable (200.403), allocable to the award (200.405), and supported by source documentation (200.302(b)(3)). The agency must maintain effective internal control aligned with the GAO Green Book or COSO (200.303(a)). Where a cost is later found non-compliant, it becomes a disallowed cost (200.410): the agency repays it from unrestricted funds, and a pattern of them becomes a Single Audit finding that carries into the agency's next award negotiation and risk designation.

The compliance paradox. The same controls that prevent disallowed costs, when applied by hand, create delay — and delay is itself a compliance problem. 2 CFR 200.305(b)(1) requires recipients to minimize the time between drawing funds and disbursing them. An agency cannot buy safety with slowness. Field-reported elapsed times for a reviewed client-assistance payment run to roughly two weeks (assumption; the Atlas records the manual loop, not a measured duration), during which an eviction notice or utility shut-off can land on the client Signet exists to protect.

The thesis for the sections that follow: whoever owns this verification-and-certification record owns the system of record for public client funds — and that record cannot be owned by an AI that decides (no agency may delegate certification to a model) nor by a generalist CRM (the controls are code that must be audited). It can be owned by a narrow system that makes verification nearly free while keeping authorization human.

Section 2

02Architectural Boundaries & Restraint-First Design

Signet is one LLM role, two deterministic engines, and a two-person human decision layer, wired so that authority flows only through the humans. The architecture is deliberately small: the smaller the surface, the easier it is for an auditor to establish what the system can and cannot do.

Why generic LLMs fail public compliance

Agentic products pitched into this market put a model in the decision seat: it reads the case, reasons over the rules, and recommends or executes a payment. They fail procurement on three grounds that do not improve with a better model:

  • Liability. GAO decision B-302789 locates pecuniary liability in the human certifying officer; 2 CFR 200.303 requires named, segregated control. An agency cannot assign that liability to a vendor's model, and no vendor will accept it.
  • Auditability. A confidence score is not a control. An auditor needs the written rule, the evidence it was applied to, and the person who acted — not a probability.
  • Governance. OMB M-25-21 requires human review and appeal for AI bearing on access to benefits; EU AI Act Art. 14 requires safeguards against automation bias. A system whose default action is "the agent approved" is precisely what these instruments constrain.

Note on model: the brief references "Claude 3.5 Sonnet"; the implementation uses a small fast model (Haiku class) for parsing and a larger model (Sonnet class) for descriptive prose, behind one swappable interface. The exact model id is configuration, not architecture.

Why non-determinism is confined to parsing

A language model is the correct tool for the only unstructured step: reading a photographed receipt with a smudged total, or a lease that names the landlord by a trade name. It is the wrong tool for every step after, because every step after must be reproducible, explainable against a written rule, and defensible to an auditor years later. So the model's output is treated as a draft of the facts, not a judgment: typed, schema-validated, confidence-bounded, and content-hash-cached. Low confidence is itself a deterministic flag that routes the item to a human. Three mechanisms make the boundary structural rather than cultural:

  • Type system. The system outcome is a two-value union (READY | ESCALATED). There is no code path from the pipeline to an approval, a denial, or a transfer; the bank adapter's transfer method is reachable only from releaseBatch, which refuses unless the batch is certified and the caller differs from the certifier.
  • Tests. Eleven engine tests run the planted anomalies through the engines with no database and no model and assert that nothing is ever approved or denied by code. A headless end-to-end check (npm run flow) fails if a payment event appears before a release.
  • Record. Every model call is logged with its cache key; every check with its evidence; the UI labels the model's role on every screen so a reviewer is never invited to treat a reading as a recommendation (the EU AI Act Art. 14 automation-bias safeguard).

03Transaction Lifecycle & the Active Learning Loop

One request, from the case manager's keyboard to the funder's report. The record is the deliverable, so each step names what it writes to the append-only, hash-chained log.

End-to-end transaction trace. Status transitions in small caps; audit event types in mono.
#StepWhat happens, and what is recorded
1RequestCase manager submits category, amount, payee-as-typed, case note, document (text or photo). The document's SHA-256 is stored. → REQUEST_CREATED. Status NEW.
2ParsingParser returns typed fields + confidence + evidence quotes; model id stored. A required field under the confidence floor becomes an EXTRACT-LOW flag. → DOC_EXTRACTED.
3Policy verificationEligibility (age, program, period); per-instance and remaining per-client caps (committed spend counts READY + APPROVED, not only released); prohibited line items; 45-day duplicate search (hash, or vendor+amount within 1%); allocation proposed in stream-priority order.
4Bank matchPayee normalized & scored against vendors + aliases; Nessie feed searched for a matching posting on reimbursements; identity status, cached public record, and (for rent) HUD 1-br FMR consulted. → CHECKS_RUN.
5Sortdecide() merges flags, applies active human rules, returns READY or ESCALATED with a grey system seal. → VERIFIED_READY / ESCALATED.
6EscalationEscalated items reach the Approver one card at a time: the document, the parsed fields, each flag with a plain title, the rule it derives from (manual §/CFR cite), a precedent, and what to look at. Approve (certify) or deny — reason required. → HUMAN_DECIDED (red seal). Denial is the only path to DENIED.
7CertificationREADY + approved items collect into a batch. A deterministic sample (seeded by batch id: 10%, min 3, plus every item ≥ $2,000) must be opened & attested before certification. → BATCH_OPENED, ITEM_ATTESTED, BATCH_CERTIFIED.
8DisbursalThe Releaser (≠ certifier) releases. Per item the bank adapter posts a Nessie withdrawal, the stream's spent balance increments, status → RELEASED. → PAYMENT_SENT, BATCH_RELEASED (both names).
9Compliance audit logEvery event appended to a hash chain (each carries the prior hash); verifyChain() recomputes end to end. Funder reports generated per stream/period, one row per request, exported to CSV.

Active Learning Loop — specification

When the Approver decides an escalation, Signet does not update a model. It reads the written reason with the chosen flag, the category, the normalized vendor, and any line-item categories, and drafts a candidate rule from a fixed template set. A rule is a small JSON predicate over a closed vocabulary of 14 fields and 8 operators (eq, neq, gt, gte, lt, lte, in, contains, matches) with all/any/not composition. Its action is one of three — and none of them approves, denies, or pays:

The three permitted rule actions. allow_within_limit can remove only a MATCH-LOW flag (a blessed alias), never a hard fail.
ActionEffect
escalateRoute a matching future request to a human.
flagAnnotate without changing the outcome.
allow_within_limitWaive a vendor-name mismatch for a confirmed alias, within the batch limit.

Audit trail structure

The log is an append-only table of events {seq, at, requestId, type, actor, payload, prevHash, hash} where hash = SHA-256(prevHash + at + type + actor + payload). From the log alone, for any released payment, an auditor can reconstruct: the source document and its hash; the fields read and the model that read them; every check and its evidence; the stream, amount and cap tested; the batch, the sample rule, and whether the item was sampled and attested; the certifier, the releaser, and the bank transaction id; and any human rule that influenced the outcome, with the reason that created it. This is the documentation 2 CFR 200.302(b) requires and the segregation the Green Book requires, held in one structure.

04Market Opportunity & Bottom-Up TAM

The beachhead is a funded budget line — compliance and finance operations — not a net-new category agencies must be taught to want.

Beachhead segments

Two buyer groups that already disburse client assistance under restricted grants and already undergo the Single Audit: the 3,143 US counties and county-equivalents, through their human-services departments, and the roughly 500 multi-service nonprofits with budgets above $100M (assumption; derived from IRS Form 990 human-services filers) that act as sub-recipients of the same federal and state money and inherit the same compliance obligations.

Monetization

A dual stream, both honoring the non-negotiable principle that the vendor holds no stake in any payment — never a percentage of funds, no fee that varies with the outcome:

  • Core SaaS license — $75,000 base, tiered by active grant streams under management ($50k ≤10 streams · $90k 11–25 · $150k 26+). The stream is the natural expansion unit: each adds a rule set, a report format, and a funder.
  • Transaction-processing fee — $1.50 per request routed through verification, flat regardless of amount, charged whether certified or denied. It funds the metered costs (parser reads, lookups, identity checks) and scales with work, not money.

05Go-to-Market, Competitive Moat & Unit Economics

Land on one high-friction grant where the pain and the audit exposure are sharpest; expand across the agency's streams as the same supervisors adopt the same Review surface.

Land & expand

Land on a single fund with written federal rules and a recent or feared finding — HUD ESG rental/utility assistance is canonical (deposit and arrears caps, FMR and rent-reasonableness, third-party-only). A 90-day pilot runs beside the current process; agency staff certify and release everything; the pilot ends with the agency's auditor reading the log. The close is the auditor's read, not a demo. Priced at the processing fee only, it stays under most small-purchase thresholds and out of a formal RFP. Expand to the second fund — the one whose supervisor sits beside the first — targeting a move from one stream to the majority of a $140M agency's ~30 (assumption) within six months, because the Review deck does not care which fund an item belongs to.

The moat: restraint + audit lock-in + a data flywheel

Once an agency produces a Single Audit from Signet's hash-chained log, that log is the record of why every client payment happened. Switching means migrating a verifiable chain into a successor or keeping Signet read-only through the retention period (three years after the final expenditure report, 2 CFR 200.334). Both are audit-risk decisions made by the finance director who signed, not software decisions. Meanwhile each human resolution accumulates as agency-owned, portable, testable rules that lower the escalation rate with tenure — simultaneously the expansion argument (less supervisor time per request), the procurement argument (the agency owns its rules), and the margin argument (a rule costs less to run than a model call).

06Hackathon Test Case & Live Demo Specification

The stress test combines two planted anomalies: a landlord who signs under a trade name the vendor list does not carry, and a rent statement that bundles a prohibited late fee. (The brief named a Zillow receipt; the synthetic workload carries no Zillow vendor, so the trace below uses the planted data as it exists. The mechanics are identical.)

The document

A case manager requests $850 of rent for a 21-year-old in the transitional-housing program and attaches a photographed statement headed "Bay Prop Mgmt". Itemization: Monthly rent $800.00; Late payment fee $50.00; Total due $850.00. The vendor list carries "Bay Area Housing LLC" (with its bank descriptor as an alias) but not the DBA.

Step trace through the pipeline for the combined-anomaly case.
StageResult
ParsingPayee "Bay Prop Mgmt" (read clearly); amount $850.00; two line items — rent $800 flagged rent, late fee $50 flagged late_fee. The flag is a category label the schema requests, not a judgment.
PolicyClient eligible (TYHF, RRDP). $850 under the rent batch limit → no LIMIT-OVER. Prohibited-items check finds a late_fee line against streams that prohibit it → POL-HARD, with the line, amount and stream in evidence. Proposed allocation: $800 rent from TYHF; the $50 is not allocable to any restricted stream.
Match"Bay Prop Mgmt" scores below the vendor-confidence floor → MATCH-LOW, best candidate Bay Area Housing LLC. Public record shows no SF registration / no web presence — expected for a private landlord, noted not flagged (manual §4.1). Rent well under 125% of HUD 1-br FMR → no RENT-HIGH.
SortTwo flags → ESCALATED. Reaches the Approver's Review deck with the photo, the parsed fields, each flag's plain title, the rule (§3.3 Prohibited items; §4.2 Names on documents), and the precedents retrieved from memory.
Human decisionSupervisor confirms the landlord by the number on the statement; writes: "Bay Prop Mgmt is Bay Area Housing LLC's DBA, confirmed by phone; $800 rent from TYHF, $50 late fee from the unrestricted fund; CM to request a fee waiver." Overrides allocation to TYHF $800 + unrestricted $50; approves. Item → APPROVED, joins the next batch. Nothing paid yet.
LearningTwo rules proposed: an alias allow_within_limit for "bay prop mgmt" above 0.95 confidence (replay: would have changed 2 past escalations on this landlord), and an escalate on late_fee (replay: matched 1, would have changed 0 — late fees already hard-fail). Finance activates the first, rejects the second as redundant; both logged.
Release & auditBatch certified after the sample is read, released by the Releaser; two Nessie withdrawals for this item (one per stream) with the batch id in the memo. The disallowed cost that would have been a finding is instead a $50 unrestricted expense with a documented reason; the next statement from this landlord matches on its alias and clears.
What the test proves. The model read a messy document correctly and did nothing else. The engines turned a trade name and a bundled fee into two specific, cited flags. A person decided in under a minute with the rule and precedent in front of them. The office learned an alias without anyone editing a config file — and declined to learn a rule it did not need. No money moved until a second person released it. Reproducible on every run with seed 1309.
